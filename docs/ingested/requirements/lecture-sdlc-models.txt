## Slide 2

```text
SDLC
```

```text
It is a process used to design, develop and test quality software.
Quality software is an application that meets customer expectations, completes within time and cost estimates. 
Software organizations use it.
 It includes a detailed plan that describes how to develop, maintain, modify, replace or enhance particular software.
 Life cycle defines a methodology for the overall development of the software process and its quality improvement. 
```

## Slide 6

```text
Waterfall Model
```

```text
It was proposed in 1970.
It was the first to formalise a framework for the software development process.
Its emphasis on requirements and design activities and on producing documentation at the early stages.
```

```text

```


## Slide 8

```text
When to Use the Waterfall Model
```

```text
 The requirements are very well documented, clear and fixed.
Product definition is stable.
Technology is understood and is not dynamic.
There are no ambiguous requirements.
Ample resources that require expertise are available to support the product.
The project is short.
```

## Slide 9

```text
Waterfall Model Advantages
```

```text
 Easy to understand and use
Easy to manage due to the rigidity of the model. Each phase has specific deliverables and a review process.
Phases are processed and completed one at a time.
Works well for smaller projects where requirements are very well understood.
Clearly defined stages.
 Well understood milestones.
Easy to arrange tasks.
Process and results are well documented. 
```

## Slide 10

```text
Waterfall Model Disadvantages
```

```text
 No working software is produced until late in the life cycle.
 High amounts of risk and uncertainty.
 Not a good model for complex and object-oriented projects.
 Poor model for long and ongoing projects.
 Not suitable for projects where requirements are at a moderate to high risk of changing. So, risk and uncertainty are high with this process model.
 It is difficult to measure progress within stages.
 Cannot accommodate changing requirements.
```

## Slide 11

```text
Disadvantages 
```

```text
 Adjusting scope during the life cycle can end a project.
Integration is done as a "big-bang. at the very end, which doesn't allow identifying any technological or business bottleneck or challenges early. 
```

```text
V-Shaped Model
```

```text
Developed to assist the project team in planning and designing for the testability of a system.
Strong emphasis on the verification and validation activities of the software.
 Also known as the Verification and Validation model.
 Testing of the software is discussed, designed, and planned in the early phases of the development.
 Execution of processes happens in a sequential manner in a V-shape.
```

## Slide 5

```text
When to Use the V-Shaped Model
```

```text
Requirements are well defined, clearly documented and fixed.
Product definition is stable.
Technology is not dynamic and is well understood by the project team.
There are no ambiguous or undefined requirements.
The project is short.

```

## Slide 6

```text
Advantages of V-Shaped Model
```

```text
Encourages verification and validation of all internal and external deliverables.  
 Encourages the definition of the requirements before designing the system.
 Easy to use.
 Enables accurate progress tracking.
 Defines product and tests each deliverable.
```

## Slide 7

```text
Disadvantages of V-Shaped Model
```

```text
 Unable to handle concurrent events.
 Unable to handle iterations of phases.
 Not capable of handling dynamic changes in requirements.
 No risk analysis activities.
```

## Slide 8

```text
Prototype Model
```

```text
Refers to developing software application prototype.
Displays the functionality of the product under development.
Enables understanding of customer requirements at an early stage of development.
An approach to designing a software prototype is as follows:
Basic requirement identification
Developing the initial prototype
Review of the prototype
Revise and enhance the prototype  
```

## Slide 9

```text
Types of Software Prototype
```

```text
 Rapid Prototyping
 Evolutionary Prototyping
 Incremental Prototyping
 Extreme Prototyping
```

## Slide 10

```text
When to Use the Prototype Model
```

```text
Prototype model should be used when the desired system needs to have a lot of interaction with the end users.
Typically, online systems and web interfaces have a very high amount of interaction with end users, and are best suited for the Prototype model. It might take a while for a system to be built that allows ease of use and needs minimal training for the end user.
Prototyping ensures that the end users constantly work with the system and provide feedback, which is incorporated in the prototype to result in a usable system. They are excellent for designing good human computer interface systems.
```

## Slide 11

```text
Advantages of Prototype Model
```

```text
 Increased user involvement in the product even before its implementation.
Since a working model of the system is displayed, the users get a better understanding of the system being developed.
Reduces time and cost as the defects can be detected much earlier.
Quicker user feedback is available, leading to better solutions.
Missing functionality can be identified easily.
Confusing or difficult functions can be identified.
```

## Slide 12

```text
Disadvantages of Prototype Model
```

```text
Risk of insufficient requirement analysis owing to too much dependency on the prototype.
Users may get confused between the prototypes and actual systems.
Practically, this methodology may increase the complexity of the system as the scope of the system may expand beyond the original plans.
Developers may try to reuse the existing prototypes to build the actual system, even when it is not technically feasible.
The effort invested in building prototypes may be too much if it is not monitored properly.
```

## Slide 2

```text
RAPID APPLICATION DEVELOPMENT (RAD)
```

```text
 Involves the user in all the phases of the life cycle.
 Based on prototyping and iterative development with no specific planning involved.
 Gathers customer requirements through workshops or focus groups.
 Uses minimal planning in favour of rapid prototyping. 
 Follows an iterative and incremental model.
Consists of small teams of developers, experts, and other stakeholders.
Develops reusable prototypes.
```

## Slide 4

```text
Advantages of RAD Model
```

```text
Changing requirements can be accommodated.
Progress can be measured.
Iteration time can be short with the use of powerful RAD tools.
Productivity with fewer people in a short time.
Reduced development time.
Increases reusability of components.
Quick initial reviews occur.
Encourages customer feedback.
Integration from the very beginning solves a lot of integration issues.
```

## Slide 5

```text
Disadvantages of RAD Model
```

```text
Dependency on technically strong team members for identifying business requirements.
Only a system that can be modularised can be built using RAD.
Requires highly skilled developers/designers.
High dependency on modelling skills.
Inapplicable to cheaper projects as the cost of Modelling and automated code generation is very high.
Management complexity is higher.
```

## Slide 6

```text
Disadvantages of RAD Model
```

```text
Suitable for systems that are component-based and scalable.
Requires user involvement throughout the life cycle.
Suitable for a project requiring shorter development times.

```

## Slide 7

```text
When to use RAD
```

```text
Depends on strong team and individual performances for identifying business requirements.
Only a system that can be modularized can be built using RAD
Requires highly skilled developers/designers.
High dependency on modelling skills
Inapplicable to cheaper projects, as the cost of modelling and automated code generation is very high.
 RAD should be used when there is a need to create a system that can be modularized in 2-3 months.
```

## Slide 8

```text
When to use RAD
```

```text
 It should be used if there’s high availability of designers for modeling and the budget is high enough to afford their cost along with the cost of automated code generating tools.
RAD SDLC model should be chosen only if resources with high business knowledge are available and there is a need to produce the system in a short span of time (2-3 months).
```

## Slide 9

```text
Iterative Model 
```

```text
Constructs a partial implementation of a total system and slowly adds more functionalities.
 Reduces the cost incurred.
 Produces an operational system quickly. 
 In each iteration, the development module goes through the requirements, design, implementation and testing phases.
 The process continues till the complete system is ready as per the requirement.
 It can be combined with other models.
```

## Slide 11

```text
Advantages of Iterative Model
```

```text
Some working functionality can be developed quickly and early in the life cycle.
Results are obtained early and periodically.
Parallel development can be planned.
Progress can be measured.
Less costly to change the scope/requirements.
Testing and debugging during smaller iterations is easy.
Risks are identified and resolved during iteration, and each iteration is an easily managed milestone.
```

## Slide 12

```text
Advantages of Iterative Model
```

```text
Easier to manage risk – The high-risk part is done first.
With every increment, the operational product is delivered.
Issues, challenges and risks identified from each increment can be utilized/applied to the next increment.
Risk analysis is better.
It supports changing requirements.
Initial Operating time is less.
Better suited for large and mission-critical projects.
During the life cycle, software is produced early which facilitates customer evaluation and feedback.
```

## Slide 13

```text
Disadvantages of Iterative Model
```

```text
More resources may be required.
Although the cost of change is lower, it is not very suitable for changing requirements.
More management attention is required.
System architecture or design issues may arise because not all requirements are gathered at the beginning of the entire life cycle.
Defining increments may require the definition of the complete system.
```

## Slide 14

```text
Disadvantages of Iterative Model
```

```text
Not suitable for smaller projects.
Management complexity is higher.
End of project may not be known, which is a risk.
Highly skilled resources are required for risk analysis.
The projects progress is highly dependent upon the risk analysis phase.
```

## Slide 15

```text
When to use Iterative Model 
```

```text
This model can be used when the requirements of the complete system are clearly defined and understood.
Major requirements must be defined; however, some details can evolve with time.
There is a need to get a product to the market early.
A new technology is being used.
Resources with the needed skill set are not available.
There are some high-risk features and goals.
```

## Slide 16

```text
Spiral Model
```

```text
Introduced by Dr. Barry Bohem and published in IEEE Computer, 1998.
Does not address changes.
Assumes relatively uniform and orderly sequence of development steps.
Encompasses the strengths of the waterfall model while including risk analysis, risk management, and support and management processes.
 Allows for the development of the product using a prototyping technique or RAD. 
```

## Slide 18

```text
Advantages of Spiral Model
```

```text
The advantages of the Spiral SDLC Model are as follows −
Changing requirements can be accommodated.
Allows extensive use of prototypes.
Requirements can be captured more accurately.
Users see the system early.
Development can be divided into smaller parts and the risky parts can be developed earlier which helps in better risk management.
```

## Slide 19

```text
Disadvantages of Spiral Model
```

```text
The disadvantages of the Spiral SDLC Model are as follows −
Management is more complex.
End of the project may not be known early.
Not suitable for small or low risk projects and could be expensive for small projects.
Process is complex
Spiral may go on indefinitely.
Large number of intermediate stages requires excessive documentation.
```

## Slide 20

```text
When to use Spiral Model
```

```text
The following pointers explain the typical uses of a Spiral Model −
When there is a budget constraint and risk evaluation is important.
For medium to high-risk projects.
Long-term project commitment because of potential changes to economic priorities as the requirements change with time.
Customer is not sure of their requirements which is usually the case.
Requirements are complex and need evaluation to get clarity.
New product line which should be released in phases to get enough customer feedback.
Significant changes are expected in the product during the development cycle.
```
